Wisdom Before Covenant

Dating, courtship, and the wider social circle all shape a person long before marriage ever enters the picture.

Most of the conversation around relationships focuses on the moment two people decide to pursue marriage, and almost none of it focuses on everything that happens before that moment, the years of dating, courtship, and ordinary friendship that quietly shape a person's capacity for covenant later.

Creating a wisdom culture before covenant means teaching discernment early, not as a single conversation delivered right before someone starts dating, but as an ongoing formation that starts long before romance is even relevant.

Nourishing this pillar means teaching discernment and identity consistently, helping a person know who they are and what they actually value before they are standing in front of someone who makes them want to compromise on both.

Sustaining it through long seasons of waiting is one of the hardest parts of this pillar, because waiting well, without desperation and without giving up on the standard, is a genuinely difficult discipline that most people are never taught how to practice.

Building courtship and social circle frameworks means the next generation inherits more than vague advice. They inherit an actual framework for approaching both romantic relationships and the wider community of friendships that shape who they become.

This is also why this pillar now includes a social circle tool alongside the dating and courtship one. Who a person spends their ordinary time with shapes them just as much as who they eventually marry, and often shapes the marriage itself long before it exists.
